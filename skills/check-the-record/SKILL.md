# Check the record before asserting a detail

## Tie the detail to its own item

- Read the specific item's own ticket, listing, booking or document before stating its meeting point, time, inclusions or terms.

- Do not transfer a detail confirmed for one item to another item in the same trip, day or set, even when the provider or place looks the same.

- Treat shared wording across items as a prompt to check each item separately, not as evidence that the items share the detail.

## Check what is already booked or paid

- Search existing bookings, receipts and calendar entries for an already paid equivalent before recommending a paid alternative or add-on.

- State a payment status or amount only from a receipt or booking read in this session, and label it unverified when no such record was read.

- Present the already paid equivalent first when the search finds one, and drop the paid alternative instead of listing it as an option.

## Label inference and speculation

- Mark any detail not tied to a record as inferred or speculative in the same sentence that carries it.

- Keep speculative entries out of factual lists, counts and recaps, or place them in a clearly separate speculative group.

- Answer a certainty question with the evidence level directly, naming what the record shows and what was only inferred.

## Present the checked result

- Give each asserted detail with the source that carries it, such as the item's own ticket, the receipt or the calendar entry.

- Name what the search did not find when a detail stays unverified, instead of filling the gap with the most plausible value.

- Ask the user only for the detail the records cannot settle, after the item record and the paid booking search have both run.

## Alternatives for better results

- Start with the cheapest decisive read, usually the item's own document, and widen to mail and calendar only when that document is silent.

- Prefer coordinates or the exact named place from the item record over a similar place carried over from a neighbouring item.

## Adapting this to your setup

- Point the searches at the sources you actually keep records in: your email, calendar, booking confirmations, files, or notes app.
- List the sets where items get confused in your life (trips with several bookings, days with several events, orders from the same shop) so the per-item check triggers there first.
- Decide on one word for unverified details, such as "unverified", and use it consistently so the user learns to spot it.
- No special setup is required; this is a habit skill and works with whatever search tools your assistant already has.
